Outsourcing vs. In-House Billing: A Cost Analysis

One of the most common questions practice owners ask is whether to handle billing in-house or outsource to a professional company. The answer depends on your practice size, specialty, and current billing performance.

The True Cost of In-House Billing

In-house billing costs include salaries (billing staff earn $40,000–$65,000/year), benefits, software licenses, training, office space, and the hidden cost of turnover. For a small practice, a single billing error can cost more than a month of outsourced service.

Benefits of Outsourcing

  • No hiring, training, or retention costs
  • Access to certified specialists across all specialties
  • Advanced technology included in service fees
  • Scalable — grows with your practice without adding headcount
  • Performance-based pricing aligns incentives

When In-House Makes Sense

Very large health systems with high claim volumes may benefit from in-house teams with dedicated technology infrastructure. For practices with fewer than 20 providers, outsourcing typically delivers better ROI.

The Bottom Line

Most practices save 20–30% on billing costs while improving collection rates when switching from in-house to outsourced billing. Request a free analysis to compare your current costs with our services.
